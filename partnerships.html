Partnerships

We do our best work with partners.

Some of the most useful things we have built for children’s services came from putting our lived and professional experience of care next to another organisation’s expertise. Why Care brought fostering and PwC brought employers. Madlug, Become and the Relational Social Work Podcast have backed our Summit and our Fellowship. We are looking for the next partners who share our purpose.

Our partners

Who we work with, and what we have done together.

Fostering

Why Care

Why Care are fostering specialists. Together we offer councils one fostering service: a diagnostic of where recruitment and retention leak, an assessment that gets applicants ready for panel in around four months, support for every new carer through their first year, and help putting England’s new fostering standards into practice. Why Care’s founder was the lead drafter of the new national standards for the Department for Education. We led the national engagement.

Employment

PwC

New World, New Skills was a day at PwC’s London office for 30 care leavers from four councils, co-branded with PwC and sent out through our networks of personal advisers. It showed what an employer can do when a council opens the door, and it shaped how we now build employer partnerships into Care to Work.

The Fellowship and the Summit

Madlug

Madlug is a social enterprise that gives a bag to a child in care for every bag it sells, so no child has to move home with their things in a bin bag. Madlug has supported The Care Leaders Fellowship, we made a podcast together, and Madlug sponsored The Care Leaders Summit.

The Summit

Become

Become is the national charity for children in care and young care leavers. Become sponsored The Care Leaders Summit, our national day with care-experienced leaders at the front.

Social work practice

The Relational Social Work Podcast

A podcast for social workers from Coventry City Council’s children’s services. We sponsored two series, because it puts relationships at the centre of practice, and the podcast sponsored The Care Leaders Summit.

What a partner gets

We bring the room, the relationships and the lived and professional experience. You bring what you do best.

1

Reach

Since 2017 we have worked with 36 local authorities and national bodies including the Department for Education and the NHS, and we are in the room with directors, virtual school heads and fostering leads every week.

2

Lived and professional experience

Care-experienced leaders and consultants who have run children’s services, so what we build together is credible with young people and with the people who commission it.

3

A product, not a press release

Every partnership so far has produced something a council can buy or a young person can use: a fostering service, an employer day, a national Summit.

Who we want to partner with

Three kinds of partner, and what each would do with us.

Delivery partners

Specialists in one part of children’s services, as Why Care are in fostering, who want a joint offer for councils with our consultancy and training behind it.

Employers

Organisations ready to open their doors to care leavers: work experience, ring-fenced roles and days like New World, New Skills, built into Care to Work.

Sector organisations

Charities, membership bodies and universities who want lived experience of care alongside professional experience in their programmes, research and events.

How a partnership starts

The same as a commission: a conversation first, and nothing agreed until it is written down.

  1. 1A conversation

    A call with Luke. What you do, what we do, and whether there is something neither of us could build alone.

  2. 2A shared purpose

    We write down what the partnership is for and who it serves. If that is not children in care and care leavers, it is not for us.

  3. 3One piece of work

    A pilot: a day, a product or a programme, so both sides see how the other works before anything is signed.

  4. 4A named partnership

    A plan for the year, how we talk about each other, and what each side puts in and gets out.
