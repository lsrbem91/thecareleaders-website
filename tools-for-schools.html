Schools

Read the behaviour, then reach for the right tool.

A practical twilight session for school staff on reading the messages in children’s behaviour and responding with the right tools. One child’s story, the theory underneath it, and tools staff use the next day. Also available as a keynote.

Length
A twilight, a full day or a keynote
How
Face to face or workshop or keynote
Group

Ask about this course
The problem it solves

Behaviour from children in care is often read as defiance and met with sanctions that escalate it. Staff want something practical they can use tomorrow, and many are unsure how social care works or who to call.

Helps withTrauma-informed practiceSchool attendance and attainmentBehaviour, incidents and restraint

What changes

  • Read behaviour as communication, not defiance, and respond in the moment
  • Try one of three practical tools in the week after the session
  • Talk about children in care differently in the staffroom and in records
  • Understand the social care side and know who to call
  • Fewer escalations and sanctions in school
  • Key adults in school who understand why the day can be hard
  • Staff who talk about them without labels or low expectations
  • School and social care working with each other, not against each other
  • How to read behaviour as communication and respond in the moment
  • What attachment and trauma look like in school

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1The messages in behaviour

Behaviour as communication, through one child’s story and what the adults around him missed. What sits behind the behaviour you see, and the window of tolerance.

You leave with The window of tolerance, as a way to read what sits behind behaviour

2Attachment and trauma

Why some children find school so hard. Attachment and trauma, and what they look like in a classroom, a corridor and a detention, and the part key adults play.

You leave with A clearer picture of the key adult’s role in the school day

3Language and stigma

How language in staffrooms, meetings and reports shapes how a child is seen and treated, and how stigma and low expectations take hold.

You leave with Ways to change the conversation about a child in the staffroom

4Where school and social care clash

Why the two systems frustrate each other, who the designated teacher really is, and what to do about it.

You leave with Three tools to take into school, with the handouts to keep

Questions commissioners ask

What will staff actually take away?

Three tools to use the next day, with handouts, and a one-page summary for staff who could not attend. At Liverpool Virtual School’s conference the tools included profile reframing, de-escalation and separating the reason from the behaviour.

What do we need to do as a school?

Release the staff team for three hours after school and bring real, anonymised examples. Afterwards, staff try one tool in the following week and tell us what happened, so the next session builds on it.

How long is it?

Three hours as a twilight session. Up to 90 minutes as a keynote.

Is it just for designated teachers?

No. It is for the whole staff team. Designated teachers who want a year-long programme should look at the Designated Leaders Programme.

Can you run it for a trust or a virtual school?

Yes, as a keynote at a conference or as twilight sessions across schools.

Is it CPD certified?

Participants receive a CPD certificate from us.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
