Luke Speaks

Keynotes and conference workshops with Luke Rodgers BEM

I went into care at 10. At 15 I was living in a B&B, and at 18 I was in a caravan. I gave my first keynote at 19, standing in front of a room of professionals and telling them what care had felt like from the inside. I’ve been doing it ever since, because it’s still one of the fastest ways I know to change how people see children in care.

That’s why I set up The Care Leaders: to go from care leaver to care leader, and to help others make the same move. Since then I’ve led the national engagement on England’s new fostering standards, been a Knowledge Equity Fellow at Saïd Business School, Oxford, and been awarded the British Empire Medal. Every talk brings together stories of care from my lived experience, the theory behind it and tools your audience can use the next day.

Living a Careless Life

What it is really like to grow up in care.

Staff and leaders make decisions about children in care every day without hearing what it is like from the inside. Language, processes and missed moments can make a child feel like a case, and nobody in the room notices. This is the talk that changes that.

By the end, delegates will

  • What children in care notice that adults miss
  • The moments that build trust, and the ones that break it
  • How systems and language can make children feel like a case
  • What one adult can change

Just Say Yes

Inspiring foster carers to say yes.

Saying yes to a child often starts with a profile full of risks, behaviours and past moves, and rarely the full story. Then carers are asked to meet needs shaped by trauma and loss without training that explains why. That is not a failure of fostering. It is a gap in how we prepare carers, and this talk bridges it.

By the end, delegates will

  • Understand how profiles shape what carers expect
  • Know the basics of trauma, attachment and behaviour as communication
  • Respond to hard moments with empathy, not fear
  • Leave with simple tools for daily care, and the confidence to say yes

The Power of the Profile

One day, the child will read what you wrote.

The profile a social worker writes becomes a child’s introduction to a school, a carer or a panel. It is often built from inherited case notes and incident logs, so the child is described by what went wrong. When the answer is no, it is the social worker who carries it. This talk is for the practitioner who knows the young person behind the paperwork.

By the end, delegates will

  • Write profiles that inspire belief, not fear
  • Reframe behaviour through a trauma-informed lens
  • Understand the emotional and ethical weight of telling a child’s story
  • Feel renewed confidence in advocating for a child

Tools for Schools

Read the behaviour, then reach for the right tool.

For a child in care, school can become another place to feel misunderstood, especially when their past arrives as a behaviour record. Most teacher training doesn’t cover trauma or children in care, yet schools are expected to be a critical support. This talk gives staff the understanding and the tools.

By the end, delegates will

  • Read behaviour as communication and respond in the moment
  • Know what attachment and trauma look like in school
  • Understand how language and stigma shape a child’s school life
  • Know where school and social care clash, and what to do about it

Lived Experience Leadership

Engaging young people in service delivery and improvement, wherever they are on the Five S framework.

Most services involve young people in the same way: a consultation, a survey, a seat on a panel. That suits some young people and leaves others out. The Five S framework gives leaders a way to see where each young person is, from telling their story for the first time to leading the work and bringing the next person through, and to offer a role that fits. This talk shows senior leaders how to build participation around that journey, so young people help deliver and improve services rather than only being asked their views.

The Five S framework

  1. Storytelling. Sharing their experience safely, on their own terms.
  2. Skills. Recognising and building the skills their experience has given them.
  3. Strategies. Helping shape plans, services and decisions.
  4. Scaling. Leading pieces of work, such as training staff or running projects.
  5. Succession. Mentoring and bringing the next young people through.

By the end, delegates will

  • Know the five stages of the Five S framework: storytelling, skills, strategies, scaling and succession
  • Recognise where the young people in their service are on it
  • Match young people to roles in service delivery and improvement that fit their stage
  • Plan how young people move through the stages, and who leads next

From Care Leaver to Care Leader

Your story as a strength, not a setback.

Care-experienced young people are often described by other people’s words: case notes, risk assessments and plans that follow them into school, housing and work. This talk helps them see that what they have lived through has built real skills, and that they can lead because of their experience, not in spite of it.

By the end, delegates will

  • See their care experience as a strength, not a weakness
  • Recognise the skills they already have
  • Feel more confident about their future
  • See themselves as people who can lead change

Be the First Yes

Opening doors for care leavers in the workplace.

Care leavers often start working life without the family networks, references and safety nets most people take for granted. Employers want to do the right thing but worry about getting it wrong. Care leavers aren’t asking for special treatment. They are asking for a chance, and this talk shows how to give one.

By the end, delegates will

  • Understand who care leavers are and what support really looks like
  • Know how to make recruitment and induction care-aware
  • Plan mentoring and onboarding that helps people stay
  • Feel confident, not cautious, about being the first yes
