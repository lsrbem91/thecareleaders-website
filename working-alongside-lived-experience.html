Voice

Co-production, not tokenism.

More services want to involve people with lived experience of care in their work. This course shows how to do it so it is safe, paid, meaningful and lasting, from people who have been on both sides: care-experienced, and professionals who have led change.

The problem it solves

Services are asked to involve care-experienced people in their work, but it often means one story at one event, unpaid, with no support afterwards. That can harm the people involved and leaves the service with little real influence to show for it.

Helps withChildren’s voice and participationCorporate parenting and leadership

What changes

  • Plan involvement with consent, wellbeing support and clear boundaries from the start.
  • Pay and credit care-experienced contributors properly.
  • Give care-experienced people a part in decisions, not only a speaking slot.
  • Build roles that carry on after a single project ends.
  • Share only what they choose, with support before and after.
  • Are paid and credited for their time and expertise.
  • See their contribution shape a decision.
  • Have routes into longer roles and leadership.
  • What lived experience adds, and what it should never be asked to carry
  • Paying, supporting and protecting people properly
  • Moving from one-off stories to shared decision making
  • Avoiding re-traumatisation in engagement

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1Why it matters

Lived and professional experience side by side: what each adds to a service, and what lived experience should never be asked to carry.

You leave with A shared view of why involvement matters, and its limits.

2Doing it safely

Wellbeing, consent and boundaries, including how to avoid re-traumatising people when they share their experience.

You leave with Safeguards to agree before anyone is asked to share.

3Doing it fairly

Pay, recognition and real influence: moving from one-off stories to shared decision making.

You leave with A clear position on pay and recognition for your service.

4Making it last

From involvement to leadership: building roles that last beyond one project, and checking your current practice against them.

You leave with A lived experience involvement checklist for your service.

Questions commissioners ask

Is this for care-experienced people or for services?

Both. It is written for leaders, commissioners and participation staff, and care-experienced adults who work in or with services take part alongside them. Hearing both views in one room is part of the point.

We already have a lived experience panel. Will this help?

Yes. The course looks at whether a panel has real influence, how members are paid and supported, and how roles can grow into leadership rather than stopping at consultation.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
