Designated Leaders Programme

Designated teachers become designated leaders.

A year-long leadership programme for designated teachers, commissioned by virtual schools. Your teachers learn practical tools chosen for their schools, learn how to teach them, and lead training for their whole staff.

Delivered by people with lived experience of being a child in care at school, and professional experience of training and leading the staff who support them.

Get the brochureTalk to us
The programme

Delegates join us as learners and leave as leaders.

Statutory guidance already makes the designated teacher a leadership role. In most schools it has become paperwork for a small number of children. This programme gives designated teachers the tools, skills and confidence to change practice across their whole school, so every teacher understands the children in front of them.

One child.Every teacher.Whole school.

What the programme sets out to do

Three objectives, for every designated teacher on the programme.

Tools

Practical tools to support young people directly, ready to use and ready to share.

Facilitation skills

The skills to plan and deliver training to the whole staff body.

Leadership

Frameworks to lead change in their school, and make the case to senior leaders.

How it works: from audit to whole-school training

The programme is built around what your schools need, not around what we already have on the shelf.

  1. 1

    The audit

    Each delegate audits needs and behaviour in their own school in the first online session.

    • A profile of need for the school’s most complex children
    • The behaviours showing up in classrooms
    • What is already working, so we build on it
  2. 2

    We read every audit

    We analyse the audits across the whole cohort.

    • The themes your schools share
    • The gaps in development and confidence
    • What matters most this year
  3. 3

    We choose nine tools

    We pick the nine tools from our library of 26 that best answer what the audits show, three for each term.

    • A toolkit built for your schools, not off the shelf
    • No two authorities get the same programme
    • You see the selection and why we chose it
  4. 4

    Delegates learn to teach them

    Delegates learn each tool, and how to train colleagues to use it.

    • The shift from designated teacher to designated leader
    • A workbook, slide deck and video for every tool
    • Rehearsal time in small groups
  5. 5

    Every school trains its staff

    Delegates take it into school, starting small and building up.

    • A short staff session in the spring
    • A full training day at September INSET
    • A plan for changing how the school responds to behaviour

What your authority gets

By the end of the year, you have more than a trained group of individuals.

  • Up to 60 designated teachers trained across the academic year
  • A profile of need for the most complex children in each school, built by the people who know them
  • Nine practical tools in use in classrooms across your authority
  • A staff training session written and delivered in each school by one of its own
  • A full training day per school, ready to run at September INSET
  • A plan for changing how each school responds to behaviour, with the case made to senior leaders
  • Training capacity built inside your schools, so you don’t buy the same training in again next year
  • Every tool, slide deck, workbook and facilitation guide, co-designed with you and handed over to keep
  • A cohort of designated teachers who know each other and share practice across the authority

Questions commissioners ask

Who is the programme for?

Designated teachers for looked after and previously looked after children. Statutory guidance says that person must be a qualified teacher, or the head or acting head.

Is it face to face or online?

Both. Three face-to-face days and three 90-minute online sessions, two a term, plus a year’s access to the resource hub.

How are places allocated?

Your virtual school holds the list and invites schools directly. We don’t take bookings from schools.

How is it paid for?

The programme is commissioned by the virtual school for the whole cohort. Ask us for an itemised cost.

What do we need to provide?

A venue for the three face-to-face days, the delegate list, and help promoting the programme to your schools. We give you a marketing toolkit, co-branded with your logo.

How do we know it worked?

The audit in the first term gives a baseline. Delegates agree how they will show impact in their own schools, and the training sessions and change plans they produce are outputs you can see.

What if a delegate misses a session?

The resource hub holds the content of every session, so they can catch up before the next one.

Can we commission it with a neighbouring authority?

Yes. Talk to us about how that would work for your region.
