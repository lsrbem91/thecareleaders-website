The programme

They join us as learners.They leave as leaders.

Every school must have a designated teacher for children in care and previously in care. It is a statutory leadership role, yet it often comes with little training and little support. This programme gives designated teachers the knowledge, the tools and the facilitation skills to pass what they learn on to every teacher, so every child in care, every child with a social worker and every child who has experienced adversity is better understood.

From teacherto leader.

What the programme sets out to do

Across the year, each teacher builds their knowledge, learns the tools, then passes them on to colleagues.

Knowledge

Understanding the children in their school: the barriers they face in education, how children develop, and the messages in their behaviour.

Tools

Nine practical tools chosen from their schools’ audits, each with a workbook, facilitation guide, slides and video.

Facilitation

How to plan training with each tool’s facilitation guide, and lead a full training day for their whole staff at September INSET.

How it works: from audit to whole-school training

The programme is built around what your schools need, not around what we already have on the shelf.

  1. 1

    The audit

    In the first online session, each teacher completes a behaviour audit and a data audit in their own school, using the previous year’s data.

    • The behaviours showing up in classrooms
    • Where children need the most support
    • What is already working, so we build on it
  2. 2

    We read every audit

    We analyse the audits across the whole cohort.

    • The themes your schools share
    • The gaps in development and confidence
    • What matters most this year
  3. 3

    We choose nine tools

    We pick the nine tools from our library of 26 that best answer what the audits show, three for each term.

    • A toolkit built for your schools, not off the shelf
    • No two authorities get the same programme
    • You see the selection and why we chose it
  4. 4

    Teachers learn to teach them

    Each teacher learns each tool, and how to train colleagues to use it.

    • The shift from designated teacher to designated leader
    • A workbook, facilitation guide, slide deck and video for every tool
    • Rehearsal time in small groups
  5. 5

    Every school trains its staff

    Teachers use the facilitation guides to plan and lead a full training day for their whole staff.

    • A full training day at September INSET
    • The audits captured again at the end, to explore what has changed
    • All 26 tools for 12 months after the programme

What your authority gets

By the end of the year, you have more than a trained group of individuals.

  • Up to 60 designated teachers trained across the academic year
  • Behaviour and data audits for every school, at the start and the end of the year
  • Nine practical tools in use in classrooms across your authority
  • A full training day per school, written and led at September INSET by one of its own
  • Training capacity built inside your schools, so you don’t buy the same training in again next year
  • Every tool, slide deck, workbook and facilitation guide, co-designed with you and handed over to keep
  • A cohort of designated teachers who know each other and share practice across the authority
  • 12 months’ access to all 26 tools for every designated teacher after the programme

Questions commissioners ask

Who is the programme for?

Designated teachers for children in care and previously in care (the statutory wording is looked-after and previously looked-after children). Statutory guidance says that person must be a qualified teacher, or the head or acting head.

Is it face to face or online?

Both. Three face-to-face days and three 90-minute online sessions, two a term, plus the online platform from the first face-to-face day and 12 months’ access to all 26 tools after the programme.

How are places allocated?

Your virtual school holds the list and invites schools directly. We don’t take bookings from schools.

How is it paid for?

The programme is commissioned by the virtual school for the whole cohort. Ask us for an itemised cost.

What do we need to provide?

A venue for the three face-to-face days, the list of designated teachers, and help promoting the programme to your schools. We give you a marketing toolkit, co-branded with your logo.

How do we know it worked?

The behaviour and data audits at the start give each school a baseline. We capture them again at the end of the programme to explore what has changed, and every school has a full INSET training day written and delivered by its own designated teacher.

What if a teacher misses a session?

The online platform holds the content of every session, so they can catch up before the next one.

Can we commission it with a neighbouring authority?

Yes. Talk to us about how that would work for your region.

When does it start?

Every programme starts at the beginning of the academic year, and runs through to September INSET.
