Leaving care and work

They were a child in care first.

For probation, prisons, police, housing, health and other adult services who work with care-experienced people aged 18 to 35. What growing up in care can leave behind, and how small changes in approach make people more likely to engage. The prisons and probation version supports the HMPPS framework for people with care experience.

The problem it solves

Many adults on probation, in custody or using housing and health services grew up in care, but it is often missing from the file. Missed appointments get read as not caring, and people disengage. This course helps staff recognise care experience and change their approach so people are more likely to engage.

Helps withCare leavers, work and the local offer

What changes

  • Ask about care experience when it is not on the file.
  • Treat a missed appointment as a reason to follow up, not a refusal.
  • Use approaches that build trust with adults who have learned to expect moves.
  • Bring in personal advisers and local offers when someone is entitled to them.
  • Staff who see the person, not only the record.
  • Appointments that feel worth turning up to.
  • Help to reach the support they are entitled to as care leavers.
  • How to recognise care experience when it is not on the file
  • How trauma and moves shape trust in adult services
  • Why missed appointments are rarely about not caring
  • Approaches that build engagement
  • Who else to involve, from personal advisers to local offers
  • Probation officers, and prison care experience leads and staff
  • Police and youth justice staff
  • Housing and homelessness teams, now that the intentional homelessness test no longer applies to care leavers
  • Health and mental health practitioners

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1The file

What the record says, and what it leaves out. How to recognise care experience when it is not written down.

You leave with Questions to ask that bring care experience into view.

2The person

Growing up in care, seen through the eyes of one young adult, so staff understand what it can leave behind.

You leave with A real story to hold in mind when reading the next file.

3The pattern

Trauma, trust and services in adulthood. How moves and trauma shape trust, and why missed appointments are rarely about not caring.

You leave with A way to read disengagement that points to what to try next.

4The approach

Practical changes for your next appointment, and who else to involve, from personal advisers to local offers.

You leave with A checklist of changes and contacts for your next appointment.

Questions commissioners ask

Does it fit the HMPPS framework for people with care experience?

The prisons and probation version is built to support it. We use examples from probation and custody so staff can connect what they learn to the framework and to their own caseloads.

We are a housing team, not justice. Is this relevant?

Yes. Housing and homelessness teams are a core audience, especially now that the intentional homelessness test no longer applies to care leavers. We use examples from your own service area.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
