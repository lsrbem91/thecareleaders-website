Leaving care and work

They were a child in care first.

For probation, prison, police, housing and health staff who work with adults aged 18 to 35 who grew up in care. Staff learn to spot care experience that is missing from the record, read missed appointments and mistrust differently, and connect people to the leaving care support they are still entitled to. The prisons version is built around the HMPPS Supporting Prisoners with Care Experience Policy Framework.

Length
Half day, about 3 hours
How
Face to face or live online
Group

For

The problem it solves

Many adults on probation, in custody or presenting as homeless spent part of their childhood in care, but it rarely shows on the file unless someone asks. A missed appointment gets logged as non-compliance and a sharp reply as hostility, and the case moves towards breach or closure before anyone checks whether the person still has a personal adviser. The rules are changing too: since 30 September 2026 care leavers under 25 can no longer be found intentionally homeless, and the HMPPS framework now asks prisons to identify care experience and, with consent, contact the local authority. Front-line staff need to know what that means for the person in front of them.

Helps withCare leavers, work and the local offer

What changes

  • Ask about care experience early and sensitively, and record it with the person’s consent.
  • Treat a missed appointment as a prompt to follow up, with a call, a reminder or a different time, not as a refusal.
  • Explain decisions plainly, and make promises that are small enough to keep.
  • Check whether the person has a personal adviser and invite them into sentence, release or housing planning.
  • Use the local authority’s local offer for care leavers to find what the person is entitled to.
  • Being asked about their history once, kindly, and not having to prove it.
  • Staff who follow up when they miss an appointment instead of closing the case.
  • A personal adviser who knows where they are and is part of the plan.
  • Housing, release and health plans that use the support they are entitled to.
  • Recognise signs that someone may have grown up in care when it is not on the file, and ask about it in a way that respects consent
  • Explain the difference between care experienced and care leaver, and who is entitled to leaving care support and a personal adviser up to age 25
  • Describe how early trauma, broken attachments and repeated moves can shape trust, memory and responses to authority in adulthood
  • Apply a trauma-informed reading of missed appointments and disengagement before recording them as non-compliance
  • Plan practical changes to appointments, letters and first conversations that make attendance more likely
  • Identify the right contacts in the leaving care team and the local offer, and when to involve them
  • Explain what the HMPPS framework for prisoners with care experience and the change to intentional homelessness rules mean for your service
  • Probation practitioners and prison offender managers
  • Prison senior leads, care experience single points of contact and keyworkers
  • Police, custody and youth justice staff who work with young adults
  • Housing options, homelessness and supported accommodation teams
  • Health, mental health and substance use practitioners
  • Up to 40 face to face. No prior knowledge of children’s social care is needed.

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1The file

We open with a real, anonymised case record and ask what it tells you and what it leaves out. Then the definitions that decide entitlement: care experienced, care leaver, and who can still get leaving care support. Staff practise asking about care in a first meeting.

You leave with A short set of questions for asking about care experience, and a note on recording it with consent

2The person

The same record, retold from the side of the young adult it belongs to, drawing on our lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. We trace what growing up in care can leave behind: many moves, relationships that ended suddenly and a habit of expecting adults to leave.

You leave with A real story to hold in mind the next time a file reads like non-compliance

3The pattern

The theory behind disengagement: how early trauma and disrupted attachment shape trust, threat responses and memory under stress, and why services built on letters and fixed appointment slots fail people who grew up with unpredictable adults. Staff map common reactions to authority against what they may be protecting.

You leave with A way to read a missed appointment or a hostile reply that points to what to try next

4The approach

Practical changes for your next appointment, and who else to involve: personal advisers, the local offer, the leaving care team, and for housing teams the new intentional homelessness rules. Groups plan changes for a real case from their own caseload.

You leave with A checklist of changes and local contacts to use at your next appointment

Questions commissioners ask

Does it fit the HMPPS framework for prisoners with care experience?

Yes. The prisons version is built around the Supporting Prisoners with Care Experience Policy Framework, issued in May 2026: identifying care experience, recording it with consent, contacting the local authority for people under 25 and involving personal advisers in sentence and release planning. It suits senior leads, single points of contact, keyworkers and prison offender managers, and sits alongside the HMPPS care experience eLearning rather than replacing it.

We work in probation, not prisons. Is it relevant?

Yes. The Ministry of Justice and HMPPS guidance Care experience matters (2023) sets out how probation, prisons and local authorities can work together for people with care experience, in custody, on release and under community supervision. We use probation examples: missed appointments, enforcement decisions and release planning.

We are a housing team. Is this relevant?

Yes. Housing staff are often the first service to meet a care leaver in crisis. Since 30 September 2026, care leavers under 25 can no longer be found intentionally homeless under the Housing Act 1996. We cover what that changes at the front desk and how to work with the leaving care team and the personal adviser.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We use examples from your setting, whether that is a custody suite, a probation office, a housing options desk or a community mental health team, and build in your local leaving care contacts and local offer.

Who delivers it?

Trainers with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. The course was designed by Luke Rodgers BEM, who grew up in care and has spent his career leading change in children’s services, and it follows our Story, Theory, Action approach.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.
