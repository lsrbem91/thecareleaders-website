Middlesbrough Council: a vision built with young people, after special measures

Middlesbrough Council was in special measures following an inadequate Ofsted judgement, and needed more than a plan. It needed a vision the whole service, and the young people it serves, could actually recognise themselves in.

Client
Middlesbrough Council
When
2020 to 2021
Who took part
Staff across children’s services, partners, and young people

The starting point

In 2020, following an inadequate Ofsted judgement, Middlesbrough Council needed a vision for children’s services that staff and young people could genuinely own, not one written for them and handed down.

What we did

  1. BrandMiddlesbrough Children MatterA name, mission, vision and values, co-created with young people and staff rather than written for them
  2. Corporate parentingSix guiding principlesCo-produced for the corporate parenting strategy, alongside a project manager, sponsor and operational lead
  3. ParticipationA rebuilt participation offerThe Participation Team rebranded and relaunched, and the MCM Young People’s Advisory Group established
  4. Leaving careA revised offerThe leaving care offer revised and relaunched for the young people who use it
  5. FilmSix films with young peopleMade to carry the vision in young people’s own words
  6. DigitalA website and a year of contentThe Middlesbrough Children Matter website launched, with a 12-month social media calendar to keep the vision visible after launch

How we worked

Story

The vision came from what staff and young people said special measures actually felt like, not from a corporate template.

Theory

Corporate parenting principles that connect a council’s statutory duty to what a child in care actually experiences day to day.

Tools

A brand, a website, a content calendar and a young people’s advisory group, so the vision had a way to keep going after we left.

What changed

Part of the improvement work, not an add-on.

Middlesbrough Children Matter gave a council in special measures a vision its own staff and young people wrote. Young people designed the logo and staff voted it in. The Participation Team relaunched with a Young People’s Advisory Group to hold the service to its commitments, the leaving care offer was revised, and a website and a year of content kept the vision visible after we left.

Built with young people

Promises, in their words

The corporate parenting principles young people co-produced, and the commitments staff made. Choose a principle, or see another commitment.

Six guiding principles, written to young people

Friends and family

You will have contact with all of the people who are important to you in your life.

Education

You will have excellent support to access the education, employment and training that you need.

Home

You will grow up in a stable and secure home arrangement that meets your needs and wishes.

Health and wellbeing

You will have easy access to specialist support for your emotional and physical health and wellbeing.

Adult life

You are supported to leave care when you are ready, and to make a positive transition to adult life.

Voice and influence

You will have a voice and opportunities to influence your plan for permanence and our wider corporate parenting offer.

Staff commitments to Middlesbrough children

  • Always listen to you, no matter what you have to say
  • Show you that I care about you
  • Not judge, and see the best in everyone
  • Be honest and open with children, even if that may seem difficult
  • Visit children and families because I want to, not because I need to
  • Do what I say I will do, and if I can’t, explain why clearly and honestly
  • Be with children when things are hard and when they are good
  • Be unconditionally welcoming, no matter who you are or where you are from
  • Make it my responsibility to find at least one thing children are passionate about
  • Celebrate great work and praise it when I see it
  • Ask myself if doing something the right way by the system is doing the right thing for children and families
  • Remember the importance of childhood and the perspective of young people
