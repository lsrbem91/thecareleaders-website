Understanding care

What it is really like to grow up in care.

A session led by people who grew up in care and went on to work in children’s services. Honest stories about what helped, what hurt and what professionals never saw, paired with what the evidence says to do about it.

Length
90 minutes to half a day. Also as a conference keynote
How
Workshop or keynote, live online or face to face
Group
Any size, from a team meeting to a conference hall
For
Schools and more
Ask about this course
The problem it solves

Staff and leaders make decisions about children in care every day without hearing what it is like from the inside. Language, processes and missed moments can make a child feel like a case, and nobody in the room notices.

Helps withTrauma-informed practiceCorporate parenting and leadership

What changes

  • Notice the small moments that build or break a child’s trust
  • Change language and habits that make children feel like a case
  • Commit to one thing they will do differently on Monday
  • Take reflection questions back to team meetings and supervision
  • Adults who notice what they are not saying
  • Being spoken to and written about as a child, not a case
  • One adult who goes a bit further for them
  • What children in care notice that adults miss
  • The moments that build trust, and the ones that break it
  • How systems and language can make children feel like a case
  • What one adult can change
  • Whole-service and whole-school audiences
  • Conferences and development days
  • Corporate parenting boards and elected members
  • New starters in children’s services

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1The story

A real story of growing up in care, told by someone with lived experience of care and professional experience in children’s services.

You leave with A clear picture of care from the child’s side

2The system

Where the system helped and where it got in the way, including how meetings, moves and language can make a child feel like a case.

You leave with Examples of system habits to look for in your own service

3The evidence

What research tells us about the patterns in the story, so the learning rests on more than one person’s experience.

You leave with The key evidence behind the story, to share with colleagues

4The ask

Each person in the room decides what they will do differently on Monday, using reflection questions teams take back with them.

You leave with Reflection questions for your team and one personal commitment

Questions commissioners ask

Who delivers it?

People who grew up in care and went on to work in children’s services, often Luke Rodgers BEM. They share their own experience alongside what the evidence says, then link both to your practice.

Does it work for elected members and corporate parenting boards?

Yes. It runs as a short session for boards and members, showing what corporate parenting decisions feel like from the child’s side and ending with what each person can change.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.
