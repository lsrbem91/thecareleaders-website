Course: A guide to good recording

Someone is going to read this.

Training and a workbook for anyone who writes about children known to children’s social care, so records are accurate, useful and written with the child in mind. Built around four activities people do first on their own, then at their table.

Written by people who have read their own care records, and professionals who have written, audited and trained on recording in children’s services.

  • FormatFace to face, as a workshop, a team session or a conference day
  • Who it is forSocial workers, early help, fostering and adoption staff, and anyone who writes case notes
  • Group sizeFrom one team to a whole conference
  • IncludesA workbook for every participant, with one section made local to your procedures
  • Built onResearched and referenced recording models, with Writing to the Child recommended

Who it is for, and what they learn

Who it is for

  • Social workers and team managers
  • Early help and family support workers
  • Fostering and adoption staff, including supervising social workers
  • Personal advisers and leaving care staff
  • Business support and anyone else who writes in a child’s record

What they learn

  • What makes a good record and what makes a poor one
  • Recording models, and why we recommend Writing to the Child
  • How to separate fact, observation and opinion and show your reasoning
  • How to build a chronology people can use
  • Language that describes rather than labels

The four activities

The workbook runs on four activities: individually first, then as a table. Choose one to see what it covers.

Activity 1

Review a case note

A real, anonymised case note. What works, what doesn’t, and what a child reading it in ten years would make of it.

Covers

  • What makes a good record
  • What makes a poor one
  • Reading as the child
Activity 2

Read the story

Analysis before action: separating what you saw from what you think, and showing your reasoning.

Covers

  • Fact, observation and opinion
  • Showing your reasoning
  • Recording decisions and why
Activity 3

Build a chronology

Chronologies people can actually use: what goes in, what stays out, and how it reads at the next review.

Covers

  • Chronologies that work
  • Significant events
  • Keeping it current
Activity 4

Write to the child

Writing to the Child, the recommended model. The same case note, rewritten for the person it is about.

Covers

  • Writing to the Child
  • Language that describes rather than labels
  • Taking it back to your team

How it is delivered

Choose the format that fits your staff and your diary.

1

A team session

90 minutes to half a day for one team, working through the four activities with their own cases.

2

A workshop

Half a day for staff from across a service.

3

A conference day

A keynote and workshops for a whole workforce, with the workbook for every delegate, as we ran for Devon in September 2026.

What you get, and what you do

What you get

  • A workbook for every participant, to use in the session and afterwards
  • One section of the workbook written for your own procedures and audit findings
  • The slides and facilitator notes if your own staff will run it again
  • A short summary of what the room said, for your practice leads

What you and your staff do

  • Bring real, anonymised case notes to work on
  • Do each activity on your own first, then discuss it at your table
  • Rewrite one record to the child before you leave
  • Take the workbook back to your team

What changes

By the end of the course.

  • Records that a child could read and recognise themselves in
  • Chronologies that help the next worker, and the next review
  • Clearer reasoning in records, so decisions can be understood later
  • One shared standard for recording across a service

Questions people ask

Is this only for social workers?

No. Anyone who writes in a child’s record, including early help, fostering, adoption and business support staff.

How long is it?

From a 90-minute team session to a full conference day. The four activities scale to fit.

Can you make it specific to us?

Yes. One section of the workbook is written for your own procedures and audit findings.

Why Writing to the Child?

Because records are read by the child, often years later. Writing to them produces records that are accurate, respectful and clear about why decisions were made.

Can our staff run it again?

Yes. We can hand over the slides and facilitator notes.
