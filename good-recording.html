Words that matter

Someone is going to read this.

Training and a workbook for anyone who writes about children known to children’s social care, so records are accurate, useful and written with the child in mind. Built around four activities people do first on their own, then at their table.

The problem it solves

Records are read later: by the next worker, at review, by inspectors and often by the child. Weak case notes mix fact with opinion, hide the reasoning behind decisions and label rather than describe. Audits pick it up, and so do children reading their files.

Helps withRecording and the words we useGetting ready for inspection

What changes

  • Separate fact, observation and opinion, and show their reasoning in records
  • Build chronologies the next worker and the next review can use
  • Write case notes as if the child will read them
  • Describe rather than label when writing about a child
  • Records they could read and recognise themselves in
  • Clear, written reasons for the decisions made about their lives
  • Language that describes them, not labels that follow them
  • What makes a good record and what makes a poor one
  • Recording models, and why we recommend Writing to the Child
  • How to separate fact, observation and opinion and show your reasoning

  • Business support and anyone else who writes in a child’s record

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1Review a case note

Spot what is good, poor or missing in a real, anonymised case note, and what a child reading it in ten years would make of it.

You leave with A shared view of what makes a good record and a poor one

2Read the story

What do I think, why, and what should I do? Analysis before action: separating what you saw from what you think, showing your reasoning and recording decisions.

You leave with A way to separate fact, observation and opinion in your notes

3Build a chronology

Turn a story into a clear chronology: what goes in, what stays out, significant events, keeping it current and how it reads at the next review.

You leave with A chronology the next worker and the next review can use

4Write to the child

Writing to the Child, the recommended model. The same case note, rewritten for the person it is about, using language that describes rather than labels.

You leave with One record rewritten to the child, to take back to your team

Questions commissioners ask

Do people work on real case notes?

Yes. Bring real, anonymised case notes. Each activity is done on your own first, then discussed at your table, and everyone rewrites one record to the child before they leave.

Can it respond to what our audits are finding?

Yes. One section of the workbook is written for your own procedures and audit findings, and we give your practice leads a short summary of what the room said.

Is this only for social workers?

No. Anyone who writes in a child’s record, including early help, fostering, adoption and business support staff.

How long is it?

From a 90-minute team session to a full conference day. The four activities scale to fit.

Can you make it specific to us?

Yes. One section of the workbook is written for your own procedures and audit findings.

Why Writing to the Child?

Because records are read by the child, often years later. Writing to them produces records that are accurate, respectful and clear about why decisions were made.

Can our staff run it again?

Yes. We can hand over the slides and facilitator notes.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
