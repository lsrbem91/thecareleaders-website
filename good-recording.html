A guide to good recording

Someone is going to read this. Training and a workbook for anyone who writes case notes about children known to children’s social care, so records are accurate, useful and written with the child in mind.

Ask about recording training

At a glance

Who it’s forSocial workers, early help, fostering and adoption staff
FormatsConference days, workshops and team sessions
IncludesA workbook to use in the session and afterwards
Made localOne local section for your own procedures and findings

What it covers

Every model in the workbook is researched and referenced.

  • What makes a good record, and what makes a poor one
  • Recording models, with Writing to the Child as the recommended approach
  • Chronologies people can actually use
  • Language that describes rather than labels

From case note to letter

A companion workbook of four activities, for individuals first and then table discussion.

  1. 1
    Review a case note

    Spot what is good, poor or missing.

  2. 2
    Read the story

    What do I think, why, and what should I do?

  3. 3
    Build a chronology

    Turn a story into a clear chronology.

  4. 4
    Write to the child

    Rewrite it as if the child will read it.
