Darlington Virtual School: the training designated teachers designed for themselves

Designated teachers in Darlington told us most of their training ticked a box and changed nothing. So they designed the alternative with us: Designated Leaders, a 10-month programme that treats them as leaders, not just liaisons.

Client
Darlington Borough Council, Darlington Virtual School
When
April 2025 to July 2026

Designated teachers from schools across Darlington

The starting point

Every school must have a designated teacher for children in care. It is a statutory role, but the training and support rarely match its weight. Designated teachers sit between the child, the school, social care and home, and are often left to find their own resources. Calvin Kipling MBE, Darlington’s Virtual School Head at the time, asked us to build a leadership programme for them, and to build it with them.

What we heard

On 10 April 2025, 8 designated teachers spent an afternoon telling us what makes training worth their time. They were clear about what doesn’t work: low-level content, buzzwords, long online sessions, and trainers with no experience of schools or social care. They wanted practical tools, time with each other, and facilitators with lived and professional experience. A follow-up survey at the end of April asked for more ideas.

What we did

  1. ListenDesignated teachers pitched the worst training programme they could imagine. We reversed each pitch into a fix, then scored every idea for impact and feasibility.
  2. DesignWe turned the highest-scoring ideas into Designated Leaders: a 10-month programme following the school year, with a face-to-face day each term, online follow-ups and a task to complete in school in between.
  3. BuildWe produced the onboarding pack, workbooks, lesson plans, slide decks and games, and gave designated teachers access to The Care Leaders Online. The Virtual School’s educational psychologist helped plan the sessions.
  4. DeliverThe first face-to-face day ran on 16 September 2025, with further days in January and April 2026. Each term’s task put the learning to work: training staff on trauma-informed behaviour, teaching tools to students, and reviewing the school’s behaviour policy.

The programme now runs as six facilitated sessions across the school year, two each term: a live online session to open the term, then a face-to-face day in the second half. Each term teaches three practical tools, nine across the year, chosen by the cohort from what their own needs and behaviour audit shows.

  1. Autumn, onlineKnow the childDesignated teachers build an anonymised profile of need for one pupil in care and complete a needs and behaviour audit in their school.
  2. Autumn, face to faceThree tools that workThe first three tools, chosen by the cohort from the audit results, taught and practised together.
  3. Spring, onlineWhat happened?Reflecting on using the tools in school and what changed for the child.
  4. Spring, face to faceBuild your trainingDesignated teachers build and rehearse a staff training session, then run a 45-minute taster in school before summer.
  5. Summer, onlineHow did it land?Reviewing the taster and planning the full training day.
  6. Summer, face to faceLead the changeTurning the training into a full day for staff and a change plan, delivered at the school’s September INSET.

How we worked

Story

Real experience of care ran through every session, the thing designated teachers said most training lacked.

Theory

ACEs, relationships, trauma and behaviour, and how trauma shows up differently in the classroom, backed by research they could reference.

Tools

Ready-made lesson plans, workbooks and games, so designated teachers could train colleagues and students, not just attend.

The programme followed what the workshop asked for. Designated teachers wanted a community, so every face-to-face day mixed teaching with time to share practice. They wanted to do something, so every term ended with a task in their own school.

What changed

Built by designated teachers, now running beyond Darlington.

The same programme now runs for Wakefield and Salford.

The Virtual School worked to widen the reach as the year went on. By the third face-to-face day, staff from what it called its hardest-to-reach secondary school were in the room, alongside a designated teacher new to the role. 22 designated teachers took part. Darlington’s Assistant Director for children’s services has since asked to build on the work started through the Virtual School.

The first tool

Separate the reason from the behaviour

Four stages, adapted from Emotion Coaching. Choose a stage.

Separating reason and behaviour

Recognise the reason

What is the feeling or need underneath? Name it before you do anything about the behaviour.

Offer support

Respond to the reason first: comfort, space, help with the thing that is hard.

Highlight the behaviour

Then talk about what happened, kept separate from who the child is.

Give ownership

Let the child own the repair and the next step, so the change is theirs.
