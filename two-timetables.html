Schools

One child, two timetables.

Children with a social worker live by two timetables: school’s and social care’s. This course brings teachers and social workers into the same room to understand each other’s worlds, and to stop the child falling into the gap between them.

Length
Half day. On demand in about an hour
How
Face to face, live online or on demand
Group
Ideally a mix of school and social care staff
For
Schools and more
Ask about this course
The problem it solves

Children with a social worker lose learning when school and social care run to different timetables: meetings in lesson time, moves mid-term, information that never arrives. PEPs become paperwork, and neither side knows what the other needs.

Helps withSchool attendance and attainmentStability and matching

What changes

  • Plan meetings and visits around lessons, not across them
  • Use the PEP as a working plan, not a form to complete
  • Share key information before a move, not after it
  • Say clearly what they need from colleagues on the other side
  • Fewer lessons missed for meetings and visits
  • A PEP that reflects what they want and need
  • Not having to repeat their story to every new adult
  • Moves that take their education into account
  • How school and social care each see the same child
  • Where things go wrong: meetings in lesson time, moves mid-term, missing information
  • Making the personal education plan a useful plan
  • What each side needs from the other, said plainly
  • Joint habits that protect a child’s education
  • Designated teachers and SENCOs
  • Social workers and personal advisers
  • Virtual school staff
  • Foster carers and residential staff who attend school meetings

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1Two worlds

A day in the life of the child, the teacher and the social worker, showing how school and social care each see the same child.

You leave with A shared picture of each other’s working day and pressures

2The gaps

Where the timetables clash, such as meetings in lesson time, moves mid-term and missing information, and who pays the price.

You leave with A list of the clash points to fix locally

3The plan

Getting real value from the personal education plan, so it becomes a useful plan for the child rather than a form.

You leave with A shared checklist for PEPs, meetings and moves

4The deal

School and social care staff agree what each needs from the other, and the joint habits that will protect the child’s education.

You leave with A written agreement on what each side will do differently

Questions commissioners ask

Do we need both school and social care staff there?

It works best with both, because hearing the other side is the point. If that is not possible, we run it for one group and bring the other side’s view in through real examples.

Will it help with PEP quality?

Yes. One part of the course is given to the personal education plan, and staff leave with a shared checklist for PEPs, meetings and moves that both sides can use.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
