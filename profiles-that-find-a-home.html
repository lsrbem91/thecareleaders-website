Words that matter

A profile is a first impression.

Referrals and child profiles decide who a carer or home says yes to. This course helps the people who write them describe a child honestly, warmly and usefully, and helps the people who read them look past the paperwork.

The problem it solves

A referral or profile is often all a carer or home sees before saying yes or no. When it lists incidents and risks without the child, good matches are missed and placements start badly or break down. The people who write profiles and the people who read them rarely train together.

Helps withStability and matchingRecording and the words we use

What changes

  • Write referrals that set out risk honestly alongside strengths, interests and the child’s views.
  • Read a profile for the child behind the incidents, not only the risk.
  • Ask better questions before saying yes or no to a referral.
  • Use one shared profile template across the team.
  • Are introduced to carers and homes as a whole person.
  • Have their own views and interests in what is written about them.
  • Have a better chance of a match that lasts.

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1The first read

What a profile says before anyone meets the child: what carers and homes look for in a referral, what puts them off, and what is often left out.

You leave with A clear picture of how a profile is read on the other side.

2Writing it

Describing risk honestly without defining a child by it, and adding the strengths, interests and voice that make a profile useful.

You leave with A profile template that holds risk, strengths and voice together.

3Reading it

Reading between the lines of a profile, spotting what is missing, and looking for the child behind the risk.

You leave with A reading checklist for any referral or profile.

4Matching

From profile to the right home: using what is written, and what you ask, to make matching decisions that last.

You leave with Questions to ask before a matching decision.

Questions commissioners ask

Should the people who write profiles and the people who read them come together?

It works best that way. Writers hear how their words are read, and carers and managers hear the pressures on the people writing. We can also run separate sessions for each group.

Does it cover fostering and residential matching?

Yes. It is for anyone who writes or reads referrals and profiles: children’s social workers, placement and commissioning teams, supervising social workers, foster carers and residential managers who make matching decisions.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
