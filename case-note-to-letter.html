Write it to the child.

A practical workshop on writing records, reports and life story pieces as if the child will read them, because one day they might. People bring a real piece of writing and leave with it rewritten.

The problem it solves

Children in care can ask to read their files, and many do as adults. Too often they find records written about them, not to them, full of jargon and judgements nobody explained. Recording quality is also a regular theme in audit and inspection.

Helps withRecording and the words we use

What changes

  • Write case notes and reports addressed to the child, in words they would understand.
  • Explain decisions in the record, including the reasons a child might disagree.
  • Record hard events truthfully, without blame or labels.
  • Use shared team templates for letters, reports and life story pieces.
  • Read their own records and recognise themselves in them.
  • Understand why decisions about their lives were made.
  • Find the hard parts of their history written kindly and honestly.
  • Why writing to the child changes what you write
  • Keeping accuracy and accountability while writing warmly
  • Explaining decisions so a child understands them
  • Writing about hard things kindly and truthfully
  • Turning a case note into a letter, in the room

  • Residential key workers

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1Read it back

We start with what care-experienced adults say when they read their files as adults, and what the words told them about how they were seen.

You leave with A clear sense of how a record lands with the child.

2The shift

Writing to the child: what changes, and what has to stay, including accuracy, accountability and the facts a record needs to hold.

You leave with Before and after examples to refer back to.

3The rewrite

Everyone brings a real piece of their own writing, with identifying details removed, and rewrites it in the room with support and feedback.

You leave with Their own case note or report, rewritten and ready to use.

4Make it stick

Turning one good rewrite into a team habit: templates, prompts, and how managers can back the change in supervision and audit.

You leave with Team templates and prompts from the workbook, ready to adopt.

Questions commissioners ask

Does writing to the child make records less professional?

No. The facts, the analysis and the accountability stay. What changes is who the record speaks to and how it explains things. Writing for a child usually makes a record clearer and more precise, not softer.

What should people bring?

A real piece of their own writing, such as a case note, a report extract or a life story piece, with identifying details removed. The rewrite works best on real work, not a made-up example.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
