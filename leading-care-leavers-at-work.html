Leaving care and work

A first job can change everything.

For line managers, supervisors and HR or apprenticeship teams who employ care leavers. Managers learn why the first 12 weeks matter so much, how to give feedback and hold standards without triggering a walk-out, and what to do when housing, money or family crises reach work, so more care leavers stay, settle and progress.

Length
Half day. On demand in five short modules
How
Face to face, live online or on demand
Group

For

The problem it solves

Care leavers can drop out of first jobs and apprenticeships in the early weeks, often for reasons their manager never sees: a housing problem, no family to fall back on for money, or a sharp reply that is really about something else. A late arrival turns into a disciplinary instead of a conversation. Employers who sign the Care Leaver Covenant’s Care Leaver Friendly Employer Charter commit to clear expectations, support such as a workplace buddy, and responding to challenges with empathy. This course shows managers how to do that day to day without lowering the bar.

Helps withCare leavers, work and the local offer

What changes

  • Plan an induction that makes a new starter feel expected from day one, with a named person to go to.
  • Give feedback and set boundaries in a way that holds the standard and keeps the person in the room.
  • Notice when housing, money or family pressures are affecting work, and have an early conversation.
  • Know where to point someone for help: their personal adviser, the council’s local offer for care leavers and, for apprentices, the care leavers’ bursary.
  • Keep the same standards while changing how support is offered.
  • A manager who understands why the first months can feel so hard.
  • Someone to go to when life outside work gets in the way.
  • Feedback that is straight and fair, given in private.
  • A fair chance to stay in work, progress and build a career.
  • Describe what leaving care can mean for a young person aged 16 to 25, including having less family back-up for money, housing and advice.
  • Explain how past trauma can show up at work, such as lateness, shutting down or a sharp reaction to criticism, and respond without escalating it.
  • Plan a 12-week induction with clear expectations, a named buddy and check-ins booked in advance.
  • Use a feedback script that names the standard, asks what is going on and agrees the next step.
  • Recognise early signs that a housing, money or family crisis is affecting work, and act before it becomes a disciplinary issue.
  • Identify the support available to a care leaver employee, including their personal adviser up to age 25, the council’s local offer and the £3,000 care leavers’ bursary for apprentices who start under 25.
  • Hold a progression conversation that agrees a realistic next step.
  • Line managers and supervisors of care leavers, including first-time managers
  • HR, recruitment and apprenticeship teams
  • Council departments employing care leavers, and businesses signed up to the Care Leaver Covenant
  • Workplace mentors and buddies
  • Up to 30 managers per group. No knowledge of care or trauma is needed.

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1Understanding

Leaving care, trauma and work. Opens with a story of a first job, drawn from our facilitators’ lived experience of care and professional experience of employment programmes. We cover what leaving care at 16 to 18 can mean, and use Dan Siegel’s window of tolerance to explain why a small criticism can feel like a big threat.

You leave with A one-page guide to what may sit behind lateness, silence or a sharp reply.

2Starting well

Induction and the first 12 weeks. How to build belonging from day one: a named person to go to, expectations written down, check-ins booked before problems start, and how a care leaver chooses whether to tell you about their care experience.

You leave with A 12-week induction checklist.

3Staying well

Supervision, feedback and wobbles. Managers practise feedback that holds the bar and keeps the relationship, then plan for when housing, money or family crises hit, including when to involve the young person’s personal adviser.

You leave with A feedback script for hard conversations, and a crisis plan with local contacts.

4Growing

Progression and long-term success. Keeping people in work through setbacks, planning the next step, and using apprenticeships and the care leavers’ bursary to support it.

You leave with A plan for progression conversations with each care leaver you manage.

Questions commissioners ask

Do our managers need to know about care or trauma already?

No. The course starts from what leaving care and trauma can mean at work, then stays with the everyday decisions managers make: inductions, supervision, feedback and what to do when a crisis hits.

Does this mean lowering standards for care leavers?

No. It is about support without lowering the bar. Managers keep the same expectations and learn how to give the extra support that helps someone meet them.

Will managers know who is a care leaver?

Only if the young person chooses to tell them. The course covers how to make that easy and what changes when they do. For apprentices, the employer’s additional funding for a care leaver is only generated if the apprentice consents to their status being shared.

Does it fit with the Care Leaver Covenant?

Yes. The Care Leaver Friendly Employer Charter asks employers to give existing staff guidance on supporting care-experienced colleagues. This course is that guidance for your managers.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
