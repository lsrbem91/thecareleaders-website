Voice

Can you show inspectors what children say?

A half day for registered managers, deputies and responsible individuals in children’s homes. You build everyday ways to hear what children think, record what changed as a result, and show it in children’s own words. Children get adults who act on what they say, and inspectors find the evidence on any day they arrive.

Group

The problem it solves

Regulation 7 of the Children’s Homes Regulations 2015 expects staff to seek children’s views, explain how those views were considered and give reasons for decisions. Many homes do the asking but not the recording: views sit in a survey or a house meeting note, with nothing to show what happened next. Ofsted’s 2026 consultation on social care inspection proposes more consistent ways for inspectors to hear children’s lived experiences directly, so that gap will show.

Helps withGetting ready for inspectionChildren’s voice and participation

What changes

  • Build hearing children into daily routines, key work and handovers, not only surveys and house meetings
  • Record each view alongside what was done about it and why, as Regulation 7 expects
  • Use children’s own words in Regulation 45 reviews and in responses to Regulation 44 reports
  • Explain to children what an inspection is and who they can talk to, without telling them what to say
  • Adults who ask what they think during ordinary days, in ways that suit them
  • Hearing back what happened after they spoke up, including the reason when the answer is no
  • Knowing how to complain and how to reach an advocate
  • Knowing what an inspector is there for, and feeling free to say what they actually think
  • Describe what Regulation 7, the children’s views, wishes and feelings standard, requires of staff
  • Explain how inspectors gather children’s experiences under the current SCCIF, and what Ofsted’s 2026 consultation proposes to change
  • Choose listening methods that fit each child’s age, communication needs and mood, beyond surveys and meetings
  • Write a record that links what a child said, what the home did and what changed, in the child’s own words
  • Use children’s views as evidence in Regulation 45 quality of care reviews
  • Plan how to prepare children for an inspection visit without coaching what they say
  • Registered managers and deputy managers of children’s homes
  • Responsible individuals, heads of service and quality assurance leads
  • Regulation 44 independent persons, who review the same evidence
  • Up to 24 people. Managers from several homes can join the same group. People need to know their home’s recording system; no other preparation is needed

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1What changes

We open with a story from lived experience of care about being asked for your views and never hearing back. Then what Regulation 7 asks, how inspectors hear from children under the SCCIF now, and what Ofsted’s 2026 consultation proposes for children’s homes, including a new area on children’s enduring relationships.

You leave with A one-page summary of what inspectors look for when they listen to children

2Listening

Methods that work in a busy home: conversations in the car or over food, key work, and tools for children who do not want to talk or who communicate differently. The theory is the Lundy model of participation: space, voice, audience and influence.

You leave with A listening plan for each child, matched to how they like to talk

3Evidence

Records that show what a child said, what the home did and what changed, written in the child’s own words. Managers rewrite an anonymised entry from their own home’s log and test it against Regulation 7.

You leave with A recording format that links a child’s view to action, and one rewritten entry from your home

4Inspection

Ready every day, not the week before. How to explain an inspection to children, make sure they can speak to an inspector privately, and why coaching backfires. Then how to bring children’s views into your Regulation 45 review.

You leave with The voice and evidence toolkit, with an action plan for your home

Questions commissioners ask

Is this based on Ofsted’s new framework?

It is based on the current SCCIF and the Children’s Homes Regulations 2015, which you are inspected against now. We also cover what Ofsted proposed in its 2026 consultation. Ofsted plans to respond in early 2027 and bring changes in between spring 2027 and spring 2028, so we update the course as the final framework is published.

Will this help children perform well for inspectors?

No, and it should not. The course is about hearing children every day so inspectors see the real picture. We cover how to prepare children for a visit without coaching what they say.

Is this only for children’s home managers?

It is written for children’s homes, their managers and responsible individuals. Heads of service, quality leads and Regulation 44 independent persons are part of the audience too, because they review the same evidence.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We use examples from your homes, your recording system and the children you care for. If you run a different kind of service, tell us and we will say honestly whether it fits.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.
