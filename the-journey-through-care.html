Understanding care

One child’s story, the whole care journey.

Our flagship course follows Sam from before he came into care to the day he left. Six sessions of 60 minutes, each pairing his story with the theory behind it, so school staff, social care teams and carers understand what life is like for children in care and what to do differently.

Length
Six sessions over six weeks
How
Live online or face to face
Group
Whole-service cohorts: Kent ran three of 145 to 174 staff
For
Schools and more

The problem it solves

Staff see the behaviour, the missed meeting and the file, but not the life behind them. That gap shows up in escalations, in records that label, and in schools and social care misreading each other while the child falls between them.

Helps withTrauma-informed practiceSchool attendance and attainmentStability and matching

What changes

  • Read behaviour as communication and respond to it, not treat it as defiance
  • Change the language they use about children in meetings and in records
  • Picture the care journey from the child’s side when planning support
  • Work across school and social care, knowing how the other side works
  • Adults who ask what happened before reacting to what they did
  • Meetings and records that describe them fairly, without labels
  • School and social care staff who understand each other’s world
  • More understanding at the hard points: entering care, moving and leaving
  • What happens to a child before, during and after care, from the child’s side
  • Child development, adverse childhood experiences, attachment, trauma and loss
  • How to read behaviour as communication and respond to it
  • The language and recording that shape a child’s life, and how to get them right
  • What school, social care and leaving care look like to the child, and how to work across them
  • Teachers, teaching assistants and designated teachers
  • Social workers, personal advisers and early help staff
  • Foster carers and residential staff
  • New starters in children’s services, and anyone who wants to understand the journey through care

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1Before care

Sam’s story starts before birth. What happens before a child comes into care, and what it does to a developing brain and body: child development, adverse childhood experiences and parenting styles.

You leave with A handout on ACEs and parenting styles, and a reflection task

2Entering care

The real reasons children come into care, the types of care (foster, residential and kinship), and what the first days, weeks and a placement move feel like from the child’s side.

You leave with A handout on routes into care and the types of care

3Attachment, trauma and loss

Attachment theory, trauma and loss, told through what Sam did and didn’t do with the adults around him. Why a child who has lost everyone finds it hard to trust.

You leave with A handout on attachment styles and relationship-focused practice

4Behaviour as communication

Emotional regulation, the window of tolerance, and what behaviour is telling you when a child can’t say it. Then what to do in the moment.

You leave with A way to read the message in behaviour and respond in the moment

5Language and recording

Someone is going to read this. The words adults use about children in care, in meetings and in records, and what it is like to read your own file years later.

You leave with A check for your own language, with writing to the child in mind

6School, social care and leaving care

Education for children in care, where school and social care clash, and what leaving care really means, including what happens at 18.

You leave with A plan for working across school and social care for one child

Questions commissioners ask

Can school staff and social care staff join the same cohort?

Yes. It was built for both, and session 6 is about where school and social care meet. Kent’s cohorts brought teachers, school staff, college staff and social care staff together.

What do staff do between sessions?

A short reflection task. We also ask each person to bring a real child, anonymised, to think about across the six sessions, so the theory lands on someone they know.

How long is each session?

60 minutes. Six sessions make the full course, usually one a week.

Is it online or in person?

Both. Live online is the most common. Face to face works as twilight sessions or a development day.

Can we have the recordings?

Yes. We record the series for your organisation and license it for a year, on your platform or ours.

Who delivers it?

Facilitators with lived experience of care and professional experience of the schools and social work teams around it. Sam’s story is told from lived experience.

How many people can join?

Whole-service cohorts work. Kent ran three cohorts of 145 to 174 staff. Licensed recordings have no limit.

Is it CPD certified?

Participants who complete the course receive a certificate of completion from us for CPD purposes.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.
