The problem it solves

Children in care are far more likely to be criminalised than other children, often for incidents any other family would handle at home. A call to the police over a broken window can leave a child with a record. This course helps homes and police respond in ways that keep children safe and out of the justice system.

Helps withBehaviour, incidents and restraint

What changes

  • Ask what a good parent would do before calling the police.
  • Use de-escalation and restorative responses that still hold children to account.
  • Work with police as partners when they are involved.
  • Apply the local protocol on reducing criminalisation consistently.
  • Incidents at home are dealt with at home, as in any other family.
  • Mistakes are repaired, rather than turned into criminal records.
  • Adults who hold them to account and keep the relationship going.

Session by session

  • Story
  • Theory
  • Action

Every part opens with a real story, adds the evidence behind it and ends with something people use the next day. Open a part to see what happens.

1The pattern

How children in care get criminalised, told through one young person’s story, and what a record can mean for their future.

You leave with A shared picture of how a child goes from incident to record.

2The moment

Incidents in the home: what happens next, and who decides. We work through what a good parent would do, before anyone calls the police.

You leave with A decision guide for incidents, to use before picking up the phone.

3The alternative

De-escalation, restorative practice and NVR in action, as responses that keep children safe and still hold them to account.

You leave with De-escalation and repair steps staff can use on shift.

4The protocol

Making your local agreement work, together. How homes and police use the national and local protocols on reducing criminalisation.

You leave with A joint protocol checklist for homes and police to agree.

Questions commissioners ask

Do we need police in the room?

It works best when they are. Joint sessions let homes and police agree how incidents will be handled before they happen. We can also run it for homes or police on their own.

Does this mean never calling the police?

No. It means not calling them by default. Staff learn when police involvement is right, and how to respond to everything else the way a good parent would, keeping children and staff safe.

Can you shape it around our service?

Yes. We tailor the examples, the length and the mix of people in the room to your service, your procedures and the children you work with.

How do you price it?

We quote per cohort, based on how many people, the format and how much tailoring you want. Tell us what you need and we will send you a clear quote.

Who delivers it?

People with lived experience of care and professional experience of children’s services. Every course is designed by Luke Rodgers BEM and follows the same Story, Theory, Action approach.
